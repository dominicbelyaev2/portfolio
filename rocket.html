NAR Level 1 High-Power Rocket Certification

Role: sole builder & flyer · Tools: OpenRocket, fiberglass kit build · Timeline: Spring 2026, certified June 2026

Built and flew a 3-inch fiberglass rocket on an Aerotech H182R, earning my NAR Level 1 high-power certification with a nominal deployment and recovery. Built from a Wildman kit flown in stock configuration — the goal of this flight was disciplined execution, not modification.

Build

  • Motor mount tube epoxied into G10 fiberglass centering rings; Kevlar shock cord anchored beneath the forward ring with an internal fillet.
  • Fins jig-aligned during bonding, then attached with syringe-injected carbon-fiber-filled epoxy fillets through holes drilled at each fin root — fillet geometry you can't reach with a stick on an internal joint.
  • Single-deploy parachute recovery via motor ejection.

Launch — Bong Recreation Area, WI

Flight simulation (OpenRocket)

  • Predicted apogee: 2,461 ft (750 m) on the H182R
  • Max velocity: 158 m/s (Mach 0.46) · max acceleration ≈ 14.7 g
  • Launch rod exit velocity: 16 m/s · time to apogee: 11.7 s
  • Ejection timing set from the sim: the simulated burnout-to-apogee coast was 10.45 s, so I drilled the motor's ejection delay down to match — simulated deployment happens 0.05 s after apogee at ~2 m/s vertical velocity, minimizing shock load on the chute and airframe.
  • Flight result: stable ascent, ejection at apogee, clean recovery — certification signed off. The cert flight carried no altimeter (single-deploy, motor ejection), so these are predictions; Flight 2 adds instrumentation to close the predicted-vs-actual loop.

What came next — the instrumented campaign

The follow-on grew into its own vehicle: a smaller 2.3-inch airframe carrying a molded carbon nosecone and a custom flight computer, flown twice in one afternoon on 15 Aug 2026 — a parachute failure diagnosed from the logged data, then a clean reflight, with boost repeating to ~2% across the pair. Full story, data, and an interactive 3D replay of both flights: Two Flights, One Afternoon.
